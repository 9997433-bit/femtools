## 1. Conclusions first

1. **`fea/reduction.py`** implements Guyan, IRS and SEREP behind one `ReductionResult`.
   Guyan's `T^T K T` reproduces the Schur complement to **6.0e-16**; IRS removes about
   five decades of the Guyan frequency bias (1.35e-03 → 4.54e-08 on a 16-sensor
   cantilever); SEREP recovers the **unmeasured** DOFs of the same cantilever to
   **6.5e-16** with a worst-case MAC of **1 − 5.6e-16** from only 8 sensors.
2. **`solve_complex_modes`** solves the damped pencil through a symmetric state-space
   linearisation. It reproduces an analytical two-DOF non-proportional system to
   **5.1e-16** and matches the Rayleigh-damping closed form for ζ to **8.8e-12**.
   The scaling step is not cosmetic: **without it the undamped check is only accurate to
   2.0e-05** (§4.2).
3. **`solve_modes` takes a complete spectrum of an SPD pencil through `scipy.linalg.eigh`.**
   The eigenpair residual drops from **3.5e-08 to 5.7e-15**, the off-diagonal of
   `Phi^T K Phi` from **5.9e-09 to 4.7e-16**, the top 20 frequencies from **4.3e-10 to
   2.1e-15**, and a driving-point FRF rebuilt from the full modal basis from **4.9e-11 to
   9.2e-14** against the direct dynamic-stiffness solve. That last pair is the
   "1e-10 vs 1e-14" issue in the brief.
4. **No golden regressed.** HEX8 rigid-body frequencies, bending ratio and patch test are
   **bitwise identical** (SHA-256 of the frequency array unchanged). BEAM2 is untouched
   (still on the ARPACK path). BAR2 moved by **one ULP** — onto the exact analytical
   value, relative error 4.05e-16 → **0.0**.

## 2. `fea/reduction.py`

```python
from femtools.fea.reduction import ReductionResult, guyan, irs, serep

T, Krr = guyan(K, master)            # terse form from REMAINING.md
r = guyan(K, master, M)              # ReductionResult with K_red and M_red
r = irs(K, M, master)
r = serep(phi, master_rows, K, M)    # K, M optional
```

`ReductionResult` carries `T`, `master`, `slave`, `K_red`, `M_red`, `method`, `meta`, and
unpacks as `(T, K_red)` so both spellings work — the same trick `AssemblyResult` already
uses for `K, M, C = assemble_km(model)`. Useful members: `expand`, `restrict`,
`expansion_error`, `reduced_modes`.

Both dense and sparse `K`/`M` are accepted and give **bitwise identical** bases
(`max|T_sparse - T_dense| = 0.0` for Guyan and IRS); the slave block is factorised with
SuperLU when sparse, Cholesky-with-LU-fallback when dense.

### 2.1 Master ordering is the caller's, not sorted

`craig_bampton` sorts its boundary set with `np.unique`. I deliberately did **not**: for a
reduction the master order *is* the row and column order of `K_red`, and for a pretest
sensor set that ordering is the one thing the caller cares about. `guyan(K, [8, 0, 8, 4])`
returns `master == [8, 0, 4]` — duplicates dropped, first-occurrence order kept — and
permuting the input permutes `K_red` consistently (verified). Boolean masks and negative
indices are accepted; empty, out-of-range and wrong-size masks raise `ValueError` with the
offending size in the message.

### 2.2 Guyan is exactly static condensation

The identity the brief asks to verify, on the 96-DOF golden cantilever
(`|T^T K T − (K_mm − K_ms K_ss^-1 K_sm)| / max|Schur|`):

| masters | slaves | `Krr` vs Schur | `T[master] − I` | `T[slave] − (−K_ss^-1 K_sm)` |
|--------:|-------:|---------------:|----------------:|-----------------------------:|
| 32 | 64 | **1.84e-16** | 0.0 (exact) | 4.14e-16 |
| 16 | 80 | **5.97e-16** | 0.0 (exact) | 2.73e-15 |
|  8 | 88 | **1.29e-15** | 0.0 (exact) | 5.66e-15 |

And the statics that follows from it, with a random load applied to master DOFs only:
master displacements agree with the full solve to **7.7e-13**, and the expanded full field
to **1.3e-14**. Guyan costs nothing at zero frequency and everything above it.

### 2.3 Guyan vs IRS: what the inertia correction buys

Relative frequency error against the exact 96-DOF solve. Masters are the two lateral
translations of every *n*-th node.

| sensor set | n_master | | mode 1 | mode 3 | mode 5 | worst of 1–10 |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| every node | 32 | Guyan | 4.61e-08 | 1.78e-06 | 1.45e-05 | 1.95e-04 |
| | | **IRS** | **9.92e-11** | **6.12e-12** | **1.09e-13** | **3.02e-10** |
| every 2nd | 16 | Guyan | 3.14e-06 | 1.44e-04 | 1.35e-03 | 2.13e-02 |
| | | **IRS** | **8.29e-11** | **7.56e-11** | **4.54e-08** | **9.32e-05** |
| every 4th | 8 | Guyan | 1.05e-03 | 1.92e-02 | 1.40e-01 | 1.12e+00 |
| | | **IRS** | **3.00e-10** | **1.16e-05** | **4.66e-03** | **1.46e-01** |

Two properties worth stating because they are testable, not just plausible:

- **Guyan frequencies are upper bounds.** `f_guyan >= f_exact` for all ten modes in all
  three sensor sets. Discarding slave inertia can only stiffen the model, so this is not
  luck; a Guyan implementation that ever undershoots has a bug.
- **IRS is not a bound.** Its errors sit on both sides. That is the price of the
  correction and it is why IRS cannot be used to bracket anything.

The 8-sensor row is the interesting one. Guyan's worst error is 112% — mode 10 is simply
not representable — while IRS still holds the first two modes at 1e-10. IRS buys roughly
"two more usable modes per sensor set", not a uniform improvement.

### 2.4 SEREP: exact recovery of the unmeasured DOFs

The check the brief asks for. Basis built from the parent modes, then the slave
(unmeasured) partition is rebuilt from the master partition alone:

| sensors | modes | slave recovery error | worst per-mode MAC on the slaves | cond(`phi_m`) |
|--------:|------:|---------------------:|---------------------------------:|--------------:|
|  8 |  6 | **4.25e-15** | 1 − 5.6e-16 | 2.08 |
| 16 |  6 | **4.88e-16** | 1 − 5.6e-16 | 1.05 |
| 16 | 10 | **6.81e-16** | 1 − 5.6e-16 | 1.14 |

Eight sensors, 88 unmeasured DOFs, machine precision. Against the same 8-sensor set the
static bases expanding one mode at a time:

| mode | frequency | SEREP(6) | Guyan | IRS |
|-----:|----------:|---------:|------:|----:|
| 1 | 4.36 Hz | **5.21e-16** | 6.87e-03 | 5.68e-06 |
| 3 | 27.34 Hz | **5.11e-16** | 1.24e-01 | 4.03e-03 |
| 6 | 108.25 Hz | **4.18e-15** | 6.13e-01 | 1.63e-01 |
| 8 | 212.16 Hz | 1.88e+00 | 1.32e+00 | 1.36e+00 |
| 10 | 350.80 Hz | 1.57e+00 | 1.18e+00 | 2.17e+00 |

Modes 8 and 10 are the honest half of the story: they are **outside the span of the
six-mode basis**, and SEREP does not merely degrade there, it is *worse* than Guyan
(1.88 vs 1.32). SEREP filters everything orthogonal to its mode set to zero, so an
out-of-band mode is not approximated, it is deleted. Guyan at least keeps a static
approximation of it. Nobody should read the top three rows without the bottom two.

Rank deficiency is reported rather than papered over: 4 sensors against a 10-mode basis
gives `meta = {"rank": 4, "exact": False, "mode_reconstruction_error": 0.696}`.

### 2.5 `reduced_modes()` and the singular reduced mass

A SEREP basis with more sensors than modes spans an `n_modes`-dimensional subspace of an
`n_master`-dimensional coordinate space, so `M_red` has exactly `n_master − n_modes` zero
eigenvalues and a plain `scipy.linalg.eigh(K_red, M_red)` fails outright with *"the leading
minor of order 7 of B is not positive definite"*. I hit this while writing the checks, and
it is a trap every caller will hit. `ReductionResult.reduced_modes()` solves the pencil
inside the subspace `M_red` actually spans and returns `(freq_hz, phi)` expanded to full
DOF space, so the null directions are dropped instead of becoming infinite frequencies.

**SEREP's reduced frequencies are exact only to about 5e-11, not 1e-15**, and that is not
a defect of the implementation. `K_red = T^T K T` is a Rayleigh quotient formed in the full
space, where `K` has a spectral spread of 3.8e7, so the round-off floor on the lowest
eigenvalue is `eps * lambda_max / lambda_1`. The *shapes* are exact to 6.5e-16; the
frequencies are limited by the conditioning of the parent, and no reduction can do better.

## 3. `solve_complex_modes`

```python
from femtools.fea.eigen import ComplexModalResult, solve_complex_modes

r = solve_complex_modes(K, M, C)          # matrices, dense or sparse
r = solve_complex_modes(assembly)         # AssemblyResult -> free partition, full-DOF modes
r.freq_hz, r.zeta, r.modes                # the frozen API
r.damped_freq_hz, r.eigenvalues, r.mpc(), r.real_modes(), r.is_underdamped
```

The linearisation is the symmetric one, `A = [[C, M], [M, 0]]`, `B = [[K, 0], [0, −M]]`
with `A z' + B z = 0`, solved as a generalized problem by QZ. Symmetric blocks keep the
conjugate pairs of a real system exactly paired, and a **singular mass** is handled as
infinite eigenvalues rather than through an explicit inverse: a 2-DOF system with one
massless DOF returns 2 roots, not the 4 a full pencil would have. `method="companion"`
gives the explicit `[[0, I], [−M^-1 K, −M^-1 C]]` form and agrees with the default to
2.0e-10 in frequency and 6.9e-11 in ζ.

### 3.1 Accuracy

| case | result |
|---|---|
| SDOF, ζ = 0.02 / 0.30 / 0.70 | f exact to 2.8e-16, ζ exact to 1.7e-16 |
| 2-DOF non-proportional vs the analytical quartic | max \|Δλ\|/\|λ\| = **5.09e-16** |
| C = 0, cantilever, first 10 vs the real solver | f to **1.25e-10**, max \|ζ\| **3.7e-13** |
| Rayleigh C, ζ vs `α/2ω + βω/2` | max relative error **8.76e-12** |
| relative residual `‖(λ²M + λC + K)φ‖` | ≤ **4.5e-16** on every case run |

### 3.2 Non-proportional damping and the MPC index

A single grounded dashpot at the cantilever tip, in the *z* direction only. The *y*-bending
modes are untouched by it, which is the control:

| damper | mode 1 (z) | mode 2 (y) | mode 3 (z) | mode 4 (y) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| c = 5 | f 4.364, ζ 0.0845, MPC 0.99966 | f 6.169, ζ **0**, MPC **1** | f 27.329, ζ 0.0135, MPC 0.99928 | f 38.660, ζ **0**, MPC **1** |
| c = 20 | f 4.393, ζ 0.3402, MPC 0.99517 | f 6.169, ζ **0**, MPC **1** | f 27.205, ζ 0.0539, MPC 0.98847 | f 38.660, ζ **0**, MPC **1** |
| c = 100 | f 1.374, ζ **1** (overdamped) | f 6.169, ζ **0**, MPC **1** | f 21.269, ζ **1** (overdamped) | f 23.418, ζ 0.2262, MPC 0.7264 |

`mpc()` is modal phase collinearity in `[0, 1]`: 1 when every DOF of a mode moves in phase
(a real mode wearing a complex scaling), falling as the phases scatter. Proportional
damping scores **1.000000000000** on every mode, which is the property that makes the index
worth having — it is the quantitative answer to "is my damping proportional in practice".

I used the **uncentred** second moment, not Pappa's mean-centred one. Mean-centring is
degenerate for a two-DOF model: any two points are collinear once their mean is removed, so
the centred index reads exactly 1 for every mode however complex. Uncentred, the same
two-mass system with one grounded dashpot scores 0.9997 / 0.987 / 0.893 as the damper goes
3 → 20 → 60 Ns/m.

### 3.3 Overdamped roots — read the caveat

`(ω_n, ζ)` describes a *conjugate pair*. It is not defined for the two unpaired real roots
an overdamped mode decays through, and the SDOF sweep shows exactly what that means: at
ζ_in = 1.5 the solver returns two real roots with `freq_hz = 1.216 Hz` (the exact
`ω_n(ζ − √(ζ²−1))/2π`, **not** the 3.183 Hz natural frequency) and `zeta = 1`. Both numbers
are correct for what they are — a decay rate expressed as a frequency — and both are wrong
if read as modal parameters. This is documented on the dataclass, `is_underdamped` tests
for it, and `include_overdamped=False` drops them.

The critically damped root (ζ = 1 exactly) is a defective double root and comes back with
**1.3e-08** relative error. That is the √eps sensitivity of a Jordan block, not something an
implementation can fix.

ζ values below `zero_tol` (1e-11 relative) are snapped to exactly zero. Left alone an
undamped mode returns \|ζ\| ~ 1e-13 with an arbitrary sign, and half of those read as
*negative* damping, which would trip every downstream stability check on a perfectly stable
model.

### 3.4 Cost

QZ on a `2n × 2n` pencil, so `O(8 n³)`:

| n_free | state size | time |
|-------:|-----------:|-----:|
| 24 | 48 | 0.9 ms |
| 48 | 96 | 2.6 ms |
| 96 | 192 | 24 ms |
| 192 | 384 | 148 ms |
| 384 | 768 | 1.52 s |

The practical ceiling is a few thousand DOFs. For anything larger the intended route is to
reduce first (`craig_bampton` or §2) and take complex modes of the reduced model — which is
what the two halves of this round are for.

## 4. `solve_modes`: direct `eigh` for a complete spectrum

### 4.1 Why shift-invert is the wrong tool for the whole spectrum

Shift-invert recovers `lambda` as `sigma + 1/mu`. For the *top* of the spectrum that means
reconstructing a large number from the reciprocal of a tiny one, and the accuracy of those
modes collapses. It is invisible when the first few modes are wanted, which is the normal
case — and it is exactly wrong when the complete spectrum is wanted, because a complete
basis is normally requested precisely because it is about to be summed back into something
that should be exact, where 1e-10 is the entire error budget.

`method="auto"` now routes `n_modes >= n_free` on an SPD pencil to `scipy.linalg.eigh`
(Cholesky reduction plus a symmetric tridiagonal solve). SPD is tested with an explicit
Cholesky of `Kff`; if it fails — a free-free model with rigid body modes — the old
shift-invert path is used unchanged. `method="eigh"` forces the direct solver and raises a
`ValueError` naming the fix if the pencil is not positive definite.

### 4.2 Measured, on the 96-DOF golden cantilever

| quantity | before (shift-invert) | after (`eigh`) | factor |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| max `‖K φ − λ M φ‖ / ‖K‖` | 3.528e-08 | **5.697e-15** | 6.2e6 |
| max `\|Phi^T M Phi − I\|` | 1.110e-15 | **5.551e-16** | 2 |
| off-diagonal of `Phi^T K Phi` | 5.888e-09 | **4.695e-16** | 1.3e7 |
| top 20 frequencies vs LAPACK | 4.328e-10 | **2.110e-15** | 2.1e5 |
| **driving-point FRF, full modal sum vs direct** | **4.928e-11** | **9.230e-14** | **534** |
| transfer FRF, full modal sum vs direct | 3.177e-12 | 1.743e-13 | 18 |

The worst shift-invert mode was #86 of 96 — the high end, as predicted. The driving-point
FRF is the sharper test of the two because every mode contributes with the same sign there,
so the errors accumulate instead of cancelling.

**One number does not improve, and it should not be read as a failure.** The *lowest*
frequencies still differ by ~1.2e-10 from an independent LAPACK call on the same matrices.
That is not the algorithm: the pencil has `lambda_max/lambda_1 = 3.8e7`, so the relative
round-off floor on `lambda_1` is `eps × 3.8e7 ≈ 8e-9`, and two different LAPACK drivers
disagree by that much. The eigensolve was never the limiting factor at the bottom of the
spectrum, and swapping it cannot help there.

### 4.3 Cost

| n_free | shift-invert | `eigh` |
|-------:|-------------:|-------:|
| 48 | 8.0 ms | **0.8 ms** |
| 96 | 8.0 ms | **1.6 ms** |
| 192 | 16.0 ms | 16.0 ms |
| 384 | 22.0 ms | 32.0 ms |

Cheaper below ~200 DOFs, 1.5x more expensive at 384. Both are already dense at this point —
the previous code also called `.toarray()` whenever `n_free < n_req + 2` — so the change
introduces no new memory risk that was not there before.

### 4.4 The Fan-Lin-Van Dooren scaling in `solve_complex_modes` (ablation)

A structural pencil in SI units is scaled catastrophically badly: this cantilever has
`‖K‖ ~ 1e9` and `‖M‖ ~ 1e-3`, twelve decades apart, and QZ balances one block into the
round-off of the other. Substituting `lambda = gamma*mu` with `gamma = sqrt(‖K‖/‖M‖)` and
rescaling by `delta` puts all three coefficient matrices at unit norm. Same code, same
model, C = 0:

| | gamma | max relative f error | spurious \|ζ\| |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| unscaled | 5.61e+04 | 2.034e-05 | 1.66e-08 |
| **scaled** | 5.61e+04 | **1.249e-10** | **3.72e-13** |

**Five orders of magnitude for six lines of arithmetic.** Without it `solve_complex_modes`
would have shipped at 1e-05 accuracy — good enough to pass a 2% tolerance test and quite
wrong.

## 6. Known limitations

1. **`solve_complex_modes` is dense-only.** A `2n × 2n` QZ caps it at a few thousand DOFs
   (§3.4). A shift-invert Krylov method for the quadratic pencil (SOAR / TOAR) is the right
   answer for large models and is not implemented.
2. **No complex-mode normalisation convention beyond "largest entry = 1".** Nastran's
   `A`-normalisation (`phi^T (2 lambda M + C) phi = 1`) is what you need to build an FRF
   from complex modal parameters directly. Not implemented; use `direct_frf` instead.
3. **IRS is single-pass.** The iterated IRS (IIRS) and the System Equivalent Expansion
   (SEREP-based) refinements are not implemented. One pass already gives 4.5e-08 on a
   16-sensor cantilever, and the iterated form needs its own convergence handling.
4. **No automatic master-DOF selection.** All three bases take the retained set as given.
   Sequential elimination by `K_ii/M_ii` ratio is the classical companion to Guyan and
   belongs with the pretest work (R4-O3's `select_exciters` neighbourhood), not here.
5. **SEREP deletes out-of-band content** (§2.4, modes 8 and 10). This is inherent to the
   method, not a bug, but it means SEREP expansion of *measured* data must be paired with a
   basis that actually spans the test bandwidth. `meta["exact"]` and `meta["rank"]` are the
   guard rails; nothing enforces them.
6. **`method="eigh"` densifies.** Requesting the complete spectrum of a 50k-DOF model will
   try to allocate a 20 GB dense matrix. The previous code had the same behaviour on the
   same input, so this is not a new hazard, but neither is it guarded.
7. **HEX8 Simo-Rifai EAS was not attempted.** It was optional in REMAINING.md, and R3-O1
   showed the real HEX8 weakness is trapezoidal distortion, which EAS does not fix either.
   The three items in the brief were worth more than a fourth formulation.

## 7. Golden hooks for R4-G1

`tests/**` is not mine, so the reproducible cases live in `femtools.fea.verification`
alongside the HEX8 ones. Every number in this report comes out of these functions —
no test needs to rebuild a model or re-derive a reference.

```python
from femtools.fea.verification import (
    beam_cantilever,              # BEAM2 model dict, same section as the golden fixture
    complete_spectrum_quality,    # {"residual", "orthogonality", "k_diagonality", "condition"}
    guyan_condensation_error,     # |T^T K T - Schur| / |Schur|
    reduction_frequency_errors,   # {"guyan", "irs", "serep"} worst relative f error
    serep_slave_recovery,         # slave-partition error and worst MAC, vs guyan/irs
)

guyan_condensation_error() < 1e-14                      # measured 5.97e-16
reduction_frequency_errors(every=2)["guyan"]            # 1.347e-03
reduction_frequency_errors(every=2)["irs"] < 1e-6       # 4.536e-08
reduction_frequency_errors(every=2)["serep"] < 1e-9     # 4.684e-11
serep_slave_recovery()["serep_slave_error"] < 1e-13     # 6.53e-16, 8 sensors / 88 slaves
serep_slave_recovery()["serep_worst_mac"] > 1 - 1e-14   # 0.9999999999999994
serep_slave_recovery()["guyan_slave_error"]             # 0.497 -- the contrast is the point
complete_spectrum_quality()["residual"] < 1e-13         # 5.70e-15 (was 3.53e-08)
complete_spectrum_quality()["k_diagonality"] < 1e-13    # 4.70e-16 (was 5.89e-09)
```

Three more worth pinning that need no new helper:

```python
solve_modes(model, n_modes=n_free).method               # contains "eigh"
solve_modes(free_free_model, n_modes=n, method="eigh")  # raises ValueError
solve_complex_modes(K, M, alpha*M + beta*K).zeta        # == alpha/2w + beta*w/2 to 1e-11
solve_complex_modes(K, M, alpha*M + beta*K).mpc()       # == 1.0 for proportional damping
```

The MPC-equals-one check is the one I would not skip: it fails the moment anyone perturbs
the linearisation or the eigenvector partitioning, and no frequency comparison would notice.

## 8. Handover notes (outside my scope)

- **`femtools/__init__.py` (R4-F1)** — `guyan`, `irs`, `serep`, `ReductionResult`,
  `solve_complex_modes` and `ComplexModalResult` are exported from `femtools.fea` but are
  not in the top-level lazy map. They are natural additions to the frozen public API.
- **`correlation/expansion.py` (R4-O3)** — `expand_serep` and `expand_guyan` should wrap
  `fea.reduction.serep` / `.guyan` rather than reimplement them. `ReductionResult.expand`
  and `.expansion_error` exist for exactly that; note §2.4 before advertising SEREP
  expansion as unconditionally superior.
- **`dynamics/craig_bampton.py` (R4-O2)** — `craig_bampton(..., n_modes=0)` and
  `guyan(K, master, M)` now compute the same basis by different code. They agree, but one
  of them should eventually delegate. Their master ordering differs (`np.unique` sorts;
  `guyan` preserves the caller's order), which is a real behavioural difference to settle
  before merging them, not a detail.
