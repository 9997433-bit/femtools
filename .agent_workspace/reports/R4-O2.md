`FreeCMSResult`: `K`, `M`, `T`, `boundary_dofs`, `interior_dofs`, `free_freq_hz`,
`residual_freq_hz`, `residual_flexibility`, `method`, `meta`; properties `n_kept`,
`n_residual`, `n_reduced`, `n_boundary`, `ndof`, `normal_modes`, `residual_modes`; methods
`interface_flexibility()`, `expand(y)`, `solve_modes(n_modes=None)`.

`PSDResult`: `psd` (real auto-spectra, `(n_out, n_freq)`), `cross_psd` (complex, opt-in),
`freq_hz`, `rms`, `outputs`, `inputs`, `response`, `meta`; properties `sigma` (= `rms`),
`three_sigma`, `variance`; methods `moment(k)`, `zero_crossing_rate_hz()`,
`peak_factor(duration_s)`, `peak(duration_s)`, `cumulative_rms()`, `index_at(f)`.

`FreeInterfaceAssembly` subclasses `ModalModel` over the *stacked physical* DOF space, exactly
like `mba.AssemblyResult`, so it drops straight into `modal_frf` / `pair_modes` / MAC.

## `cms_free` — what the two methods actually are

The whole method is one idea: the truncated free modes are missing the interface flexibility
of the deleted modes, so put it back. The residual flexibility for unit interface loads,

    G_d = K^-1 - sum_{r kept} phi_r phi_r^T / omega_r^2,     Psi_a = G_d[:, b]

is already implemented in `residuals.residual_vectors`, inertia relief and all, so `cms_free`
calls it rather than re-deriving it. That decision is worth stating because it is what makes
the reduced model diagonal: `residual_vectors` hands back the residual directions *already*
mass-orthonormalised and K-diagonalised, and they are M- and K-orthogonal to the kept modes
analytically (`Phi_e^T M K^-1 = Lambda^-1 Phi_e^T` kills the cross term exactly). So with
`T = [Phi_k | Psi]`,

    T^T K T = diag(Lambda_k, Lambda_res),     T^T M T = I

and the *only* difference between the two classical methods is one block:

* **Rubin** keeps the residual inertia. `M = I`. The deleted-mode receptance is then matched
  to second order, `G_d + omega^2 G_d M G_d + O(omega^4)`.
* **MacNeal** neglects it: the residual rows and columns of `M` are zeroed. The residual
  coordinates respond statically and contribute exactly the first-order residual flexibility.

The projection is computed numerically (`T^T K T`, not the analytic diagonal) and the measured
mass coupling `max|M[kept, res]| / max|diag M|` is reported in `meta["mass_coupling"]` instead
of being assumed: 5e-12 on the chain, 1.3e-8 on the beam. If a future basis breaks the
orthogonality, the number says so rather than the answer silently drifting.

Three things needed care:

**A singular mass matrix is a feature, not a failure.** MacNeal's massless residual
coordinates make `M` semi-definite by construction, so `scipy.linalg.eigh(K, M)` is not
available. `_semidefinite_modes` rotates into the eigenbasis of `M`, condenses the massless
block statically (Schur complement — a coordinate with no mass follows the massive ones), and
solves what is left against a diagonal, strictly positive mass. The regular case goes through
the same path, so there is one code path, not two. For a *free* component this correctly
returns the retained normal modes again: the residual set only earns its keep once something
loads the interface, which is why `free_interface_assembly` exists.

**Rigid-body truncation is silently wrong, so it is refused.** Inertia relief needs *every*
rigid-body mode; keeping 5 of 6 gives a shifted operator that is still singular and a residual
flexibility that is quietly garbage. When `cms_free` solves the basis itself it solves
`n_modes + 1` modes and raises if mode `n_modes + 1` is still rigid. That is the cheapest
possible proof and costs one extra Lanczos vector.

**A complete basis produces a *convincing* fake residual.** With every mode kept, `G_d` is
round-off — 7e-17 on the chain — but `residual_vectors` mass-normalises whatever it is given,
and normalisation destroys the evidence: the returned vector has unit mass, a plausible
pseudo-frequency (2.08 Hz) and an apparent interface compliance of 3.8e-4 against a true
4.7e-4. Every criterion computed *after* normalisation accepts it. The decision therefore has
to be taken on the raw `G_d[b, :]`, compared with the compliance the retained modes already
carry (`_significant_residuals`). Below `tol` relative, the residual set is dropped whole.

`free_interface_assembly` stacks the reduced components, writes compatibility on the physical
DOFs the generalised coordinates expand to (`B @ T`), eliminates rigid ties through the null
space (reusing `mba._nullspace`), adds elastic ties as rank-one terms, and condenses whatever
is left massless. Interface DOFs appear once per component in the result and carry identical
motion — checked to 1e-10 in the tests.

## Verification — `cms_free`

40 masses, `m = 1.7`, `k = 4.3e4`, grounded at the left end, cut at mass 20 with the cut mass
split half-and-half. Component A (20 DOF) is grounded, component B (21 DOF) is free-free and
carries a rigid-body mode, so both the regular and the inertia-relief paths are exercised in
one case. The two halves add up to the parent matrices exactly (asserted in the fixture), so
every error below is modal truncation and nothing else.

Max relative error over the first 8 assembled frequencies (parent: 0.982 … 14.519 Hz):

| free modes per half | Rubin | MacNeal | no residual (plain MBA) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 1.20e-3 | 7.04e-3 | 2.95e-2 |
| 6 | **2.76e-4** | 3.76e-3 | 2.25e-2 |
| 8 | 3.37e-5 | 1.64e-3 | 1.50e-2 |
| 12 | 1.21e-6 | 5.79e-4 | 7.74e-3 |

Rubin converges roughly an order of magnitude per two added modes; MacNeal converges too but
one order slower, which is the expected price of dropping the second-order inertia term. Both
sit inside "a few percent" from 5 modes per half — a 14-coordinate model of a 40-DOF chain.

Also verified: a Rubin component's `solve_modes()` fed to the *existing* `modal_based_assembly`
reproduces `free_interface_assembly` to 1e-8 (no new coupling machinery is required for
Rubin); the complete-basis case is exact to 6.7e-13 with zero residual modes; sparse
`csr_matrix` input takes the shift-invert-below-zero path and reproduces the dense reduction
to 1.2e-14; a
stiff elastic tie (`1e5 k`) converges to the rigid-tie answer to 1e-4 and a soft one (`0.05 k`)
lowers every frequency; and `interface_flexibility()` equals `K^-1[b,b]` minus the retained
modal compliance to 1e-8.

On the 16-element cantilever of `tests/conftest.py` (96 free DOF, assembled `Kff`/`Mff`), tip
`uy` receptance over the 0.2–0.8 retained band with 6 modes and `beta` damping:

| basis | rel L2 vs `direct_frf` |
|---|---|
| 6 free modes | 8.05e-3 |
| the same 6 modes + 1 Rubin residual mode | **8.52e-5** |

That is the `docs/CONTRACT_API.md` modal-vs-direct metric improved 94× by one extra vector,
which is the practical argument for residual flexibility in one line.

## Verification — `random`

`S_uu(f) = H(f) S_ff(f) H(f)^H`, one-sided, `f` in Hz, so `sigma_o^2 = integral S_oo df` and
RMS is the 1-sigma level. Ground truth for an SDOF under white noise is
`sigma^2 = S0 / (8 zeta omega_n^3)`, equivalently Miles.

* 25 Hz, `zeta = 0.02`, `S0 = 3e-3 N^2/Hz`, 40 k geometrically spaced lines to 2.5 kHz:
  RMS 6.955379427e-5 against 6.955379332e-5 exact — **1.4e-8** relative.
* The residual is the integration grid, not the synthesis. Uniform grids leave 6.1e-4 (8 k
  lines) and 2.5e-5 (24 k), i.e. clean `df^2` convergence; the error is on the *shoulders* of
  the resonance, not the peak, which is why geometric spacing wins so heavily. Both the
  behaviour and the fix are documented in the module docstring and pinned by a test.
* Rice upcrossing rate comes out at 24.9968 Hz where the theory says exactly `f_n = 25` Hz.
* Davenport peak factor: 2.77 (1 s), 3.98 (60 s), 4.52 (600 s).
* Independent end-to-end check: a 300 s record of the same SDOF driven by discrete white noise
  (`time_history`, 1 kHz sampling, force variance `S0 fs / 2`) gives a sampled RMS 0.7 % from
  the predicted one, with ~2 % expected statistical scatter. This is the Wiener-Khinchin
  round trip, and it is what would catch a factor of 2, a one-sided/two-sided slip or an
  `f`-versus-`omega` error in the integration — none of which the closed form alone would.

Also verified: `psd = |H|^2 S_ff` exactly for one input; uncorrelated inputs add their
variances; fully coherent inputs equal a single input driving the summed FRF column; the
`cross_psd` matrix is Hermitian and its diagonal is `psd`; all five uncorrelated force-PSD
spellings (scalar, per-frequency, per-input, per-input-spectrum, constant matrix) agree;
accelerance PSD is the receptance PSD times `omega^4`; `cumulative_rms()` ends exactly at
`rms`; an existing `FRFResult` can be reused instead of re-synthesising; and the error paths
(negative auto-spectrum, non-Hermitian matrix, wrong shape, missing `freq_hz`, re-specifying
what an `FRFResult` already fixes, bad Miles arguments) all raise with a specific message.
