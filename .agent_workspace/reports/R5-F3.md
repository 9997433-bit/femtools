## 2. The two fixes (and why they are usage fixes, not tolerance hacks)

### 2.1 `cms_rubin.py` — coupling convention

The example was written against the frozen spec, which promised `FreeCMSResult`
coordinates ordered `[boundary..., modal...]` like `CraigBamptonResult`, and it coupled
the two superelements primally on the leading 6 coordinates. The merged
`femtools.dynamics.cms_free` orders coordinates `[kept free-interface modes...,
residual modes...]` — **no physical DOF among them** — and ships
`free_interface_assembly`, which ties the *physical* interface DOFs (rows of `T`)
rigidly via null-space elimination and condenses MacNeal's massless residual block.
The primal stack silently added modal coordinates of A to modal coordinates of B, hence
the 37–43 % errors.

Fix: Rubin/MacNeal now couple through `free_interface_assembly` with six rigid ties
`("A", dof_a, "B", dof_b)`; the Craig–Bampton baseline (whose coordinates genuinely are
boundary-first) keeps the local primal `couple()` harness. The `reduced_km` shim for
never-landed `.K_red/.M_red` field names was dropped. Tolerances unchanged (Rubin 1e-2,
MacNeal 3e-2, CB 1e-2); measured errors are 2–5 orders inside them.

### 2.2 `h1_ssi.py` — SSI model-order headroom

The example called `ssi_cov(data, fs=FS, order=6)` — the bare-minimum state count for 3
modes, following the frozen spec where `order` counted pole *pairs*. In the merged
`femtools.mpe.ssi`, `order` is the **state dimension** and the *ceiling of a
stabilization sweep* (orders 2..order, poles kept only when they stabilize across
orders with `min_count=2`). With only orders {2, 4, 6} to sweep, the clustering
returned a single stabilized pole. Fix, following the kernel's own docstring example:
`ssi_cov(sim.data, fs=FS, order=20, n_modes=3, f_range=BAND)`. All three poles are
recovered: f errors ≤ 0.10 %, ζ errors ≤ 11 %, MAC ≥ 0.9998. Part 1 (H1/H2/coherence)
needed no changes. Gates unchanged (2 % / 50 % / 0.9).
