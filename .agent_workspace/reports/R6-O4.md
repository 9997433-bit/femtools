## 1. `femtools.updating.uq`

```python
from femtools.updating.uq import parameter_covariance, monte_carlo_update, UQResult
```

`parameter_covariance(jacobian, residual_cov=None, *, weights=None, residual=None,
mean=None, prior_cov=None, regularization=0.0, rcond=1e-12, parameter_names=None)`
implements the Friswell–Mottershead first-order propagation

```
Cov(theta) = (J^T W J)^-1 (J^T W C_r W J) (J^T W J)^-1
```

with three documented collapses: `W = C_r^-1` (the default when `residual_cov`
is given) reduces the sandwich to the minimum-variance `(J^T C_r^-1 J)^-1`; with
no covariance at all the residual sets the scale through
`sigma^2 = r^T W r / (n_r - n_theta)`; and `prior_cov` adds `C_theta^-1` to the
information matrix, which turns the result into the posterior covariance of the
Bayesian branch of `update_model`. The Jacobian argument accepts a bare matrix,
a `SensitivityResult` or a whole `UpdateResult` (from which the residual, the
converged parameters and the names are read).

`monte_carlo_update(model, parameters, targets, n, *, seed, ...)` re-runs the
whole `update_model` loop `n` times. `seed` is keyword-only **without a
default**, so omitting it is a `TypeError`. Three resampling schemes:
`perturb="targets"` (Gaussian measurement noise, the default),
`"residual"` (residual bootstrap around the nominal fit) and `"parameters"`
(scattered start points, which probes uniqueness rather than noise).

`UQResult` carries `mean`, `covariance`, optional `samples`, and derives
`std`, `variance`, `correlation`, `coefficient_of_variation`, `interval(level)`
(empirical percentiles when samples exist, Gaussian otherwise), `percentile`,
`to_dict` and `summary`.

### Measured

Linear reference problem `r = A p`, `A` 5×2, sigma = 0.02, `weights="unit"`:

| quantity | first order | Monte Carlo (400 samples, seed 11) | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| std(p1) | 0.00932553 | 0.00978508 | 1.049 |
| std(p2) | 0.01541854 | 0.01542453 | 1.0004 |
| corr(p1,p2) | −0.51503 | −0.52407 | — |
| mean | (1, 2) exactly | (0.999854, 2.000459) | — |

The analytic covariance reproduces the closed form `sigma^2 (J^T J)^-1` to
**0.0** absolute deviation, and the 400-sample Monte Carlo estimate agrees with
it to within 5 % — inside the ~7 % (`sqrt(2/400)`) sampling error of a variance
estimate at that sample size. 400 re-updates take **0.11 s** (2.995 Gauss–Newton
iterations per sample on average).

Two-parameter beam with a 10 % E error, measurement error 0.5 % of each
frequency: converged `theta = (1.1, 1.0)`, first-order
`std = (0.01237, 0.01685)`, `corr = -0.723`, and the 95 % interval
`[1.0758, 1.1242] x [0.9670, 1.0330]` brackets the truth. The Monte Carlo
counterpart (40 samples, seed 31, 0.2 % relative noise) gives
`mean = (1.10024, 0.99969)`, `std = (0.00440, 0.00593)` in 0.25 s. With
`perturb="parameters"` and a 15 % start scatter every sample lands on
`(1.1000000, 1.0000000)` with max std **3.8e-07**, i.e. the clean problem is
demonstrably unique.

## 2. `femtools.optimization.shape`

```python
from femtools.optimization.shape import shape_optimize, ShapeResult
```

Design variables are coordinate **offsets** of selected nodes (`directions`
accepts `"xyz"`, `"x"`, `"xy"`, a per-node sequence or a `{node: "xy"}` map), so
`x0 = 0` and the move limits are symmetric. Objectives: `"frequency"` (maximise
the `mode`-th elastic frequency), `"compliance"` (minimise `u^T f`, with a
`loads=` argument), or any callable `f(model) -> float`. The solve goes through
the existing `size_optimize` wrapper, so SLSQP (default) and trust-constr, the
design-variable scaling, the constraint scaling and the iteration history come
for free. The caller's model is never mutated; `ShapeResult.model` is the
optimised deep copy.

Both mesh barriers from the brief are implemented:

* **`min_quality`** (default 0.2) constrains `min_e m_e(x)/m_e(x0)`, where `m_e`
  is the element length for bars/beams, the minimum of the signed corner areas
  and the signed Newell area for shells (signed about the *initial* normal, so a
  fold flips the sign), the signed volume for TET4 and the minimum corner
  Jacobian determinant for HEX8. Collapse drives the ratio to 0 and inversion
  makes it negative, so one scalar covers both. Exposed publicly as
  `element_size_ratios(model, reference)`.
* **`smoothing`** adds the Laplacian energy `sum_i |x_i - mean(neighbours)|^2/h^2`
  to the normalised objective.

### Measured

| case | objective | start | optimum | change | min size ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| two-bar arch, move node 2 in y, limit 0.2 | f1 | 282.335 Hz | **1140.632 Hz** | **+304.0 %** | 1.1125 |
| same arch, 1 kN transverse load | compliance | 0.604189 | **0.033275** | **−94.49 %** | 1.1125 |
| wall–bar–mass–bar–wall, `min_quality=0.4` | f1 | 666.619 Hz | **916.336 Hz** | **+37.46 %** | **0.400000** (bound active, violation 0.0) |
| 2×2 QUAD4 clamped plate, move centre node in x,y | f1 | 8.7094 Hz | 8.8613 Hz | +1.74 % | 0.5969 |

The arch is the honest shape case: moving one apex node turns a nearly straight
bar chain into an arch, so the +304 % is a genuine geometric stiffening rather
than a discretisation artefact. The mass chain is the barrier case: maximising
`f1 = sqrt(EA(1/L1 + 1/L2)/m)` wants to collapse the short bar outright, and the
optimiser stops **exactly** at the quality bound (0.400000) with zero constraint
violation instead of inverting it. Dragging the plate's centre node to
`(0.5, -0.4)` past its own edge is reported as size ratios
`[-0.8, 1.0, -0.8, 1.0]` — two folded elements, correctly signed. Runtimes:
0.02 s (arch), 0.68 s (plate, 7 finite-difference re-analyses per gradient).

## 3. `femtools.mpe.ssi.ssi_data`

```python
from femtools.mpe.ssi import ssi_data
```

Data-driven SSI in the Van Overschee & De Moor (1996) N4SID class. Past and
future block Hankel matrices are built by the new public `block_hankel`,
stacked and factorised as `[Y_p; Y_f] = L Q`; the orthogonal projection
`Y_f / Y_p = L21 Q1` is then split by a truncated SVD of `L21` alone, which is
legitimate because `Q1` has orthonormal rows and therefore changes neither the
singular values nor `U`. Everything after the SVD — the shift-invariance
least-squares estimate of `A`, the pole acceptance window, the multi-order
stabilisation sweep and the mode shapes — is now literally the same code as
`ssi_cov`: I extracted it into a private `_identify` that both functions call, so
they return the **same `ModalParameterResult` type** with the same `extras` keys
(`ssi_data` adds `n_columns`). Reference channels (`ref_channels`, used for the
past block) and CVA weighting (inverse Cholesky factor of the future-block
covariance) are supported, as in `ssi_cov`.

`ssi_cov`'s numerics are unchanged by the refactor: `tests/test_round4_mpe.py`
and `examples/h1_ssi.py` pass with identical output.

### Measured

Noisy SDOF, 7.5 Hz, zeta = 2 %, 4 channels, 200 Hz, 200 s, 5 % noise, **seed 17**,
`order=12, n_modes=1, f_range=(1, 40)`:

* f = **7.508855 Hz**, error **0.118 %**
* zeta = 0.02153 (truth 0.02)
* **MAC = 0.99999998** against the truth shape
* 0.12 s

Noisy 2-DOF, 5 and 13 Hz, zeta = 2 %, 6 channels, 256 Hz, 240 s, 5 % noise,
**seed 23**, `order=20, n_modes=2, f_range=(1, 60)`:

| variant | f1 [Hz] | f2 [Hz] | error | MAC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ssi_data` (unweighted) | 4.99966 | 13.01442 | 0.0068 % / 0.111 % | 1.000000 / 0.999909 |
| `ssi_cov` (reference) | 4.99932 | 13.01014 | 0.0135 % / 0.078 % | 1.000000 / 0.999912 |
| `ssi_data(weighting="cva")` | 5.00006 | 13.01504 | 0.0013 % / 0.116 % | — |
| `ssi_data(ref_channels=[1,3,4])` | 5.00039 | 13.01117 | 0.0078 % / 0.086 % | 1.000000 / 0.999817 |

All well inside "a few percent with MAC > 0.95"; `ssi_data` and `ssi_cov` agree
with each other to better than 0.04 %. 0.30 s for the 2-DOF record.

## Scope notes

No DAQ, no OP2/RST/ODB, no changes under `cms/`, `fea/` or `io/`, and
`src/femtools/__init__.py` is untouched (F1 owns it — `parameter_covariance`,
`shape_optimize` and `ssi_data` are importable from
`femtools.updating` / `femtools.optimization` / `femtools.mpe` and can be
promoted to the top-level `_EXPORTS` there). Public literature only:
Friswell & Mottershead (2005) for the covariance propagation and Monte Carlo
updating, Haftka & Grandhi (CMAME 1986) and Haftka & Gürdal for node-based shape
optimization with mesh-quality control, Van Overschee & De Moor (1996) for
SSI-DATA.

Deliberately out of scope and documented as such in the module docstrings:
MCMC/Bayesian model selection and interval updating (UQ); CAD-parameter or
morphing parameterisations, analytic shape sensitivities and remeshing (shape);
combined deterministic–stochastic identification with measured inputs and pole
uncertainty quantification (SSI).
