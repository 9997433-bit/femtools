# R5-O1 — FEA kernel audit (`src/femtools/fea/**`)

Branch `cursor/r5-o1-fea-audit-ef30`, based on `cursor/femtools-remaining-d551`.
Scope: confirm the goldens still hold, fix only bugs that were actually reproduced,
keep the `modes_complex` alias.

**Result:** goldens hold. Two defects reproduced, one fixed outright and one
reported at runtime because its real fix is a change to the assembly contract
rather than to the FEA kernel. Suite went from 65 passed / 3 skipped to
**75 passed / 3 skipped**; `ruff check` clean.

## 2. Fixed: `solve_static(enforced=...)` on an unconstrained DOF

`fea/static.py`. Enforcing a displacement on a DOF the assembler had left in
the free set moved its column to the right hand side but left its own
equilibrium row in the solved set. The DOF was therefore solved as if it were
free — producing exactly the value that cancels the right-hand-side term — and
the enforced value was then written over the result. The damage is not local:
every other DOF is wrong too.

Three equal springs in series, node 1 grounded, node 3 driven to 30 mm:

| DOF | exact | before | after |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| node 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| node 2 | 0.015 | 5.6e-19 | 0.015 |
| node 3 | 0.030 | 0.030 | 0.030 |
| node 4 | 0.030 | 0 | 0.030 |

The fix takes the held DOFs out of the solved set and factors the corresponding
sub-block, and reports their reactions alongside the SPC ones (`reactions()`
gained an `extra_dof` keyword; it is only called from `solve_static`). Models
whose enforced DOFs are all SPC'd — the HEX8 patch test, the only in-tree user —
take the unchanged path and their numbers are bit-identical.

Verified: displacements and reactions exact, reactions sum to zero, equilibrium
holds on every remaining free row, enforcing 0.0 now genuinely holds a DOF at
zero, works per column for multi-load-case right hand sides, and an enforced
tip *rotation* on a beam behaves the same way. Five regressions in
`tests/test_round5_o1_fea.py`.

## 3. Reported: a tilted flat shell keeps a drilling mechanism

`fea/assemble.py`, `fea/elements/shell.py`, `fea/verification.py`.

A flat shell has no genuine stiffness about its own normal, so TRIA3/QUAD4 add
a deliberately rank-deficient drilling penalty (uniform rotation of an element
about its normal is free, which is what keeps the rigid body modes exact) and
the assembler deletes the drilling DOFs that receive nothing else. Deleting
works only while the normal *is* a global axis. Rotate the same plate so its
normal is oblique and the drilling direction becomes a combination of `rx`,
`ry` and `rz`; no whole DOF can be deleted without taking a genuine bending
rotation with it, so the per-element null spaces line up into one global
zero-energy mechanism.

Same 3x3 plate, free-free, rigidly rotated:

| | axis-aligned | oblique |
| --- | --- | --- |
| free DOFs | 80 | 96 |
| drilling DOFs removed | 16 | 0 |
| zero frequencies | 6 | **7** |
| first elastic frequency | 34.412029 Hz | 34.412029 Hz |

The elastic spectrum is identical to twelve digits — only the count of zeros
differs, which is precisely why the extra mode reads as a modelling error
rather than as a solver artefact.

I did not silently redesign this. Two candidate in-design fixes were measured:

* deleting, per node, the global rotational DOF nearest the normal
  over-constrains badly (f7 goes 34.41 -> 48.22 Hz);
* rotating the drilling direction out of each node's rotational triad
  reproduces the aligned spectrum exactly (34.412, 53.089, 69.909, 94.966,
  94.966, 182.83 Hz).

So the correct fix is the nodal frame transformation, and the reason it is not
available is that `AssemblyResult.free_dof` is an *index set*: it can delete
DOFs but cannot rotate them. Giving it a constraint transformation changes the
meaning of `free_dof` and of `modes` for every downstream consumer
(correlation, pretest, reduction, expansion), and the round-1 design notes
explicitly chose the rank-deficient penalty over an Allman-type drilling DOF.
That is a contract decision for a later round, not a bug fix.

What I did instead: `assemble_km` now rebuilds the candidate mechanism — every
drilling node rotating by the common normal, read off the dominant eigenvector
of the busiest nodal block of the penalty — and warns when its strain energy is
still zero. Calibration, as relative energy `v'Kv / (k_scale v'v)`:

| mesh | relative energy | verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| flat oblique, QUAD4 / TRIA3 | 1e-19 / 1e-21 | **warns** |
| flat oblique, one clamped edge | 1e-5 | quiet |
| flat oblique, 1 degree warp | 9e-8 | quiet |
| folded 35 deg, QUAD4 / TRIA3 | 1.7e-3 / 4.0e-6 | quiet |
| cylinder | 3.9e-3 | quiet |
| axis-aligned (x-y and x-z) | drilling DOFs deleted, candidate empty | quiet |

Four decades of margin around the 1e-12 threshold, and it only runs for shell
models that kept drilling DOFs, so the axis-aligned common case never pays for
it. `shell_plate` and `shell_drilling_orientation_gap` in `fea/verification.py`
carry the reproduction; five tests, including a no-false-positives case over
aligned / clamped / folded meshes.

## 4. Checked and correct — no change made

Roughly 130 independent analytical probes. Everything below matched to the
tolerance shown, so nothing was touched.

* **Elements.** BAR2, TRUSS2D, BEAM2 (Euler-Bernoulli and Timoshenko, both
  bending planes, orientation vector honoured), TRIA3 (CST + DKT), QUAD4
  (bilinear membrane + MITC4), TET4, HEX8 (all three formulations), MASS,
  SPRING (scalar / axial / grounded / vector coefficient), DAMPER.
* **Frame invariance.** BEAM2, HEX8, TET4 spectra invariant under a random
  rigid rotation to 2e-11 or better. QUAD4/TRIA3 are the section-3 exception.
* **Assembly.** K and M exactly symmetric and positive semi-definite for every
  element type; total translational mass exact to 6e-16 for all six; Rayleigh
  `C = aM + bK` exact; lumped mass conserves total mass and sits within 0.08%
  of consistent for the first four beam modes; `element_filter`, `on_unknown`
  raise/skip/warn, membrane-only properties all behave.
* **Geometry guards.** A folded HEX8 (Jacobian sign change inside the element)
  is rejected; consistently reversed node ordering is accepted and gives
  identical modes to 8e-16.
* **Eigensolver.** `auto` / `dense` / `eigsh` / `eigh` agree with a direct
  `scipy.linalg.eigh` within the pencil's own conditioning at every size tested
  (n_el 16/40/80, condition 3.8e7 to 2.4e10; the largest disagreement, 4.6e-8,
  is 1/1000 of the `eps * cond` budget and `method="eigh"` — no shift at all —
  shows the same error, so it is conditioning, not the shift). `_auto_sigma`
  was suspected of being too large for a beam (|sigma| reaches 2.8x lambda_1 at
  n_el=80) but sweeping it three decades either way changes nothing. Rigid body
  modes exact to 0.0 Hz for beam, shell, HEX8 and TET4; `n_modes` clipping,
  `shift` / `freq_shift_hz` / `sigma`, and `refine=False` all correct;
  orthogonality 4.4e-16; `mass_normalize` idempotent with a deterministic sign.
* **Complex modes.** SDOF at zeta 0 / 0.05 / 0.4 exact; overdamped roots follow
  the documented contract (`zeta == 1`, `freq = |lambda|/2pi`, flagged by
  `is_underdamped`, and the geometric mean of the two real roots recovers the
  undamped `wn` — my first probe read them as modal parameters, which the
  docstring explicitly warns against); Rayleigh damping reproduces the real
  modes with MPC exactly 1 and the analytical modal zeta to 4.6e-11; a
  non-proportional 2-DOF case matches a dense companion reference to 3e-16 with
  6e-17 residual; a singular mass yields infinite roots that are dropped rather
  than returned; expansion onto the full DOF space from an `AssemblyResult` is
  correct.
* **Reduction.** Guyan equals the Schur complement to 2e-14 and its frequencies
  are strict upper bounds; IRS improves on it by five decades; SEREP is a left
  inverse to 1.9e-15 (an apparent 7e-3 error was my own element-wise relative
  metric dividing by near-zero mode entries); sparse and dense inputs and
  boolean masks all agree exactly.
* **Loads.** All five documented spellings — tuple keys, per-node sequences,
  `fx..mz` records, `vector` records, dense arrays — produce the same vector.

Four probe "failures" that survive in my scratch harness are harness artefacts,
diagnosed above: under-constrained models (a rod chain and a point mass are
genuinely mechanisms laterally), a mode-family picker that grabbed the wrong
member, the overdamped contract, and the conditioning budget.

## 5. Follow-up for a later round

Give `AssemblyResult` an optional per-node rotational frame so drilling
rotations can be *rotated* out rather than deleted. That removes the warning
added here and makes shell results orientation-independent, but it changes the
meaning of `free_dof` and `modes` for correlation, pretest, reduction and
expansion, so it needs an owner across those modules rather than a kernel-local
patch.
